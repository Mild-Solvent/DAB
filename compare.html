DAB next to the messengers you know

Where DAB is ahead

Things no mainstream messenger can match, because they all need an account system and a company server to work.

No phone number, no account

Your identity is a key made on your phone. Signal, WhatsApp and Telegram all still tie you to a phone number, even where usernames now hide it from the people you chat with. There is no DAB account to hack, subpoena, or ban.

Your social graph stays yours

WhatsApp and Messenger encrypt what you say but Meta still sees who you talk to and when. DAB’s relays see unrelated throwaway keys writing to opaque tags that change every day. There is no list of your friends anywhere but on your phone.

Undiscoverable by design

Nobody can find you by number, name or search. A friend exists in DAB only after you scan their QR code or open their link — which also means no spam, no strangers, no message requests.

No company to fail you

Messages travel through free public Nostr relays run by unrelated operators — each chat uses three of six, rotating daily, so no single operator holds a history. If one disappears the others carry on, and if we disappear, your chats keep working.

Nothing stays on servers

A message is overwritten with an empty tombstone the moment your friend’s phone confirms it, with a 7-day expiry as a backstop. Disappearing messages expire on the relay too, not just on the two phones. Your history lives on your two phones and nowhere else.

No business model on you

No ads, no analytics, no AI reading along. The one routine request DAB makes to a company is a daily update check to GitHub that carries nothing about you — and one switch turns it off.

What DAB gives up

The honest other half. Some of this is the price of having no server; some of it is simply not built yet.

No forward secrecy yet

Each friendship uses one fixed key pair, where Signal and WhatsApp ratchet to a new key with every message. If someone steals your phone’s key and has recorded your traffic, they can read it. Wiping relays on delivery shrinks that window; it does not close it. No post-quantum protection either.

Your friends see your IP

To be fast without a server, DAB opens direct connections to every friend while the app is open, so each friend learns your network address, and the relays see it too. On home Wi-Fi that points at your household. A VPN fixes it; the big apps route through their own servers instead.

One-to-one only

No group chats, no group calls, no voice messages, reactions, replies, edits, typing indicators or read receipts. Everything the table marks red under Everyday messaging is something you will notice in the first week.

Calls and files have limits

With no relay server for media, calls fail on some strict networks — DAB says so instead of quietly routing you through a stranger’s server. Files stop at about 1.5 MB, because every byte crosses the peer-to-peer path with no CDN behind it.

Notifications are best-effort

Real push needs Google’s or Apple’s push servers, and a push server knows who is messaging whom. On Android DAB runs its own opt-in background listener instead; aggressive battery savers can still stop it. On iPhone there is nothing that can wake a closed app.

Young, small, unaudited

Android only, installed from an APK, one phone per identity, no desktop app. The source is not public yet and nobody independent has reviewed the security. And your friends have to install it too — WhatsApp’s three billion users are a feature DAB cannot copy.

Should you use DAB? If you want to text and call a close circle with no company in a position to know about it, yes — that is exactly what it is for. If you need groups, big files, an iPhone, or protection from a well-resourced adversary today, use Signal; if you need no phone number and that level of maturity, look at SimpleX or Threema.

How this was put together. DAB’s column comes from reading the app’s code, the same way PRIVACY.md was written; the other columns from each project’s own documentation and announcements, checked on 3 October 2026. It covers DAB as a texting and calling app — the map and location sharing are left out on purpose, since none of the others have them and they become optional in a future version. Today DAB carries messages over public Nostr relays; the planned dab-node network will change the infrastructure rows, and this page will change with it.
